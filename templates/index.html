Methodology & Documentation
Raw Telemetry & Structural Baseline
Layer 1 · Orthographic Fingerprint

Every text leaves a physical signature in its character distribution, sentence rhythm, and word-length variance — patterns the conscious author rarely controls.

AI Probability Combines burstiness σ and lexical entropy into a 0–100% score. Values above 65% indicate statistically flat text consistent with LLM output. Burstiness σ Standard deviation of sentence lengths. Natural human writing is bursty (high σ). A σ below 2.0 is a strong AI-generation flag. Lexical Entropy Shannon entropy of the word distribution. Low entropy means repeated, predictable vocabulary — a sign of constrained or templated authorship.

The Driver Matrix (section C) shows which specific words were the heaviest contributors to each psychological cluster, ranked by weight and Z-score divergence.

Dissonance Radars
Layer 2 · Macro / Micro Conflict Detection

The engine separates cognition into two parallel channels: the Micro layer (subconscious letter-physics) and the Macro layer (conscious semantic framing). When these channels diverge beyond the threshold, a dissonance event is logged.

Micro · Subconscious BPV (Bio-Psycho-Vibrational) scores derived from letter shapes, pen pressure, and kinetic energy. Reflects the writer's autonomic state rather than deliberate intent. Macro · Conscious Semantic cluster scores across 6 axes — Power, Resources, Visibility, Temporal, Cognitive, Kinetic. Reflects deliberate word choice and conscious framing. Dissonance Alert Fires when |Z_macro − Z_micro| exceeds the Δ threshold. Three classifications: Psychological Fracture (>4σ), Posturing (macro > micro), or Suppressed Signal (micro > macro).
The Waveform Physics
Layer 2.5 · Oscilloscopic Signal Display

The text is treated as a physical signal. Each character's BPV value contributes energy to a running waveform — the same way audio amplitude is visualised on an oscilloscope.

Global Waveform 100-bucket bezier-smoothed envelope of the entire document's BPV energy. Flat lines suggest uniform authorship; sharp peaks indicate emotional escalation. Micro Oscilloscope 256-point raw BPV trace of the current analysis window — no smoothing. Allows detection of rhythmic patterns, repetition artefacts, or templated structure. Structural Morse Punctuation events plotted as a binary waveform. Dense punctuation clusters correlate with high emotional arousal or deliberate syntactic control. Wavelength Telemetry Full-document energy split across dominant frequency bands. Imbalance between low / mid / high BPV bands reveals tonal suppression or register manipulation.
Psychological Payload — VAD Lexicon
Layer 4 · Emotional Dimension Analysis · NRC-VAD

The NRC Valence-Arousal-Dominance (VAD) Lexicon (Saif Mohammad, 2018) assigns every common word three independent scores on a 0–1 scale, representing the three fundamental axes of human emotional experience as defined by Russell's Circumplex Model and Mehrabian & Russell's PAD space.

Valence (V) The pleasantness of the emotional state. 0.0 = maximally negative (grief, rage, terror); 1.0 = maximally positive (joy, love, serenity). Tracks the author's conscious hedonic framing — their world-view in this text slice. Arousal (A) The activation energy of the emotional state. 0.0 = calm, sleepy, bored; 1.0 = highly agitated, excited, panicked. Arousal above 0.65 is a strong stress indicator — cross-reference with Micro Intensity and Dissonance Events. Dominance (D) The author's perceived control over their situation. 0.0 = submissive, powerless, controlled; 1.0 = dominant, authoritative, in command. Low dominance combined with high arousal is a reliable panic / anxiety signature. High dominance with negative valence indicates controlled aggression rather than despair. σ (Volatility) The population standard deviation across all matched words. A high σ means the text spans a wide emotional range — possible code-switching, mixed authorship, or deliberate register manipulation. A low σ on low-mean Arousal confirms sustained emotional flatness (suppression or AI-generated text). Coverage % The percentage of non-stop content tokens found in the lexicon. Values below 20% should be treated with caution — either the text is very short, highly technical, or the language lexicon translation is incomplete.

How to read the V/A Scatter Plot
Each dot represents a single matched word plotted at its Valence (X-axis, left = negative) and Arousal (Y-axis, bottom = calm) coordinates. Dot colour encodes Dominance: dark blue = low (submissive), cyan = high (dominant). Hover any dot to see the word and its exact V/A/D scores.

Top-left quadrant High arousal + negative valence = Panic / Distress zone Top-right quadrant High arousal + positive valence = Excitement / Joy zone Bottom-left quadrant Low arousal + negative valence = Depression / Sadness zone Bottom-right quadrant Low arousal + positive valence = Calm / Contentment zone

Assessed State block
The engine compares the three mean scores against a rule-set derived from empirical PAD research and outputs a plain-language psychological assessment. Key thresholds: Arousal > 0.65 with Dominance < 0.40 flags Panic; negative Valence (< 0.35) with high Arousal and high Dominance flags Controlled Aggression. Always cross-reference the assessed state with the Macro Semantic Radar (conscious framing) and the Dissonance Events (where micro and macro signals diverge) before drawing analytical conclusions.

Ousiometric Payload — PDS Framework
Layer 4b · Power-Danger-Structure · Dodds et al.

The Ousiometrics framework (Dodds, Minot, Iniguez, Bhattacharya, Reagan & Danforth) mathematically rotates the three raw NRC-VAD axes into a new coordinate system that is dramatically more useful for tactical intelligence analysis than raw Valence-Arousal-Dominance scores. The rotation is derived by applying Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) to the full NRC-VAD word cloud, exposing three dominant semantic axes that humans actually use to categorise threat, authority, and risk:

Power (P) Perceived authority, dominance, and influence. Loads heavily on the NRC Dominance dimension.
–1.0 = Weak / Submissive  ·  +1.0 = Powerful / Dominant.
High Power combined with low Danger = benevolent authority. High Power combined with high Danger = apex threat. Danger (D) Threat level: a function of negative Valence under activation. Computed as –Valence × 0.70 + Arousal × 0.30 (all centred to [–1, +1] first).
–1.0 = Safe / Benevolent  ·  +1.0 = Dangerous / Threatening.
Danger supersedes raw Valence for threat assessment because it separates passive sadness (low Arousal) from active threat (high Arousal + negative Valence). Structure (S) Order, composure, and predictability. Defined as the inverse of Arousal (–Arousal, centred).
–1.0 = Unstructured / Chaotic  ·  +1.0 = Structured / Composed.
Low Structure signals arousal-driven linguistic disorganisation: fragmented sentences, topic shifts, emotional leakage. High Structure indicates deliberate, rehearsed, or templated authorship. σ (Volatility) Standard deviation across all matched words on each PDS axis. High σ on the Danger axis indicates a text that alternates between safe and threatening vocabulary — possible code-switching, mixed authorship, or deliberate emotional manipulation.

Why PDS supersedes VAD for intelligence analysis
Raw Valence alone cannot distinguish between grief (low V, low A, low D) and rage (low V, high A, high D) — both are "negative" but they represent radically different threat states. The Danger axis resolves this by combining inverted Valence with Arousal, isolating activated negativity (threat) from passive negativity (sadness). Similarly, Power separates authoritative communication (high D, high V) from desperate aggression (high D, low V).

Reading the Plane of Meaning scatter plot
Each dot represents one matched word plotted at its (Power, Danger) coordinates. Dot colour encodes Structure: amber = chaotic/aroused, green = composed/ordered. Hover any dot to see the word, its PDS scores, and the underlying raw VAD values.

Top-Right (+P, +D) Apex Threat — Powerful and threatening. Maximum operational priority. Bottom-Right (+P, –D) Benevolent Authority — Powerful and safe. Leadership, reassurance, or de-escalation framing. Top-Left (–P, +D) Desperate Panic — Weak and threatened. Subject in acute distress or duress; possible coercion indicator. Bottom-Left (–P, –D) Submissive / Neutral — Low agency, low threat. Compliance, withdrawal, or sanitised/AI-generated register.

The Tactical Assessment block beneath the scatter applies a magnitude-aware quadrant classifier to the text's mean PDS score. Magnitude (distance from origin) indicates intensity: scores near the origin suggest a neutral or mixed register, while scores above 0.65 magnitude warrant heightened analytic attention. Always cross-reference against the Dissonance Events (where the subconscious Micro signal diverges from conscious Macro framing) and the VAD Arousal σ (inter-word volatility).

Layer 3 · Somatic Cipher & FFT
Archetypal Letter Profiles & Steganographic Frequency Analysis

The Somatic Cipher maps the letter distribution to a 5-axis archetypal profile: Sovereign (authority), Origin (grounding), Kinetic (movement), Resonant (connection), Liminal (transition). These archetypes derive from the somatic geometries of the letterforms themselves.

Tier Badge T1 (Universal) to T5 (Extreme Cipher) — how far the letter distribution deviates from natural language norms. T4+ may indicate intentional encoding. Quersumme Digit-sum of the dominant BPV scores — a single-digit compression of the text's psychoacoustic fingerprint, used in comparative analysis. FFT Harmonics Fast Fourier Transform of the BPV waveform. Dominant frequency peaks can reveal periodic encoding, templated sentence injection, or machine-inserted steganographic payloads.

A flat or single-peaked FFT spectrum is normal for human writing. Multiple sharp harmonics above the noise floor suggest structured non-human authorship or covert data embedding.
